# Research

My research interests lie primarily in theoretical and computational cosmology. I am interested in how cosmic structure forms and evolves, how observations of galaxies and the cosmic microwave background can be used to test cosmological models, and how signatures of new physics may appear in astronomical data.

My work has involved analytic calculations, numerical modeling, statistical forecasting, detector development, and observational instrumentation. Below is a selection of my current and previous research projects.

## University of Arizona

### [Cosmological Structure Formation and UV Luminosity Functions]({{ '/research/rhct-structure-formation/' | relative_url }})

*August 2025–Present*  
**Advisor:** Prof. Fulvio Melia

My current research examines the formation of cosmic structure and the predicted ultraviolet luminosity function of high-redshift galaxies in different cosmological models, with particular emphasis on the \(R_{\rm h}=ct\) universe.

I developed an end-to-end numerical pipeline connecting the linear matter transfer function to the matter power spectrum, halo mass function, halo growth, star formation, dust attenuation, and the resulting galaxy UV luminosity function. The framework allows predictions from alternative expansion histories to be compared consistently with those of the standard \(\Lambda\)CDM model.

My contributions include:

- Computing linear transfer functions and matter power spectra across cosmological models.
- Deriving halo mass functions and reproducing published numerical results as validation tests.
- Converting published observational measurements to a common reference cosmology.
- Connecting halo growth to star-formation rates and dust-attenuated UV luminosities using established galaxy-formation prescriptions.
- Investigating the sensitivity of predicted luminosity functions to star-formation efficiency, intrinsic scatter, and other modeling assumptions.
- Developing reproducible Python notebooks and documented numerical workflows for the full calculation.

**Methods and tools:** Python, numerical integration, cosmological perturbation theory, matter power spectra, halo mass functions, galaxy luminosity functions, statistical comparison.

## GSI Helmholtz Centre for Heavy Ion Research

### Nuclear Shell-Model Calculations for Astrophysical Processes

*DAAD RISE Professional Fellow, June 2023–January 2024*  
**Theory Department**  
**Advisor:** Dr. Thomas Neff

At GSI, I worked on computational nuclear theory relevant to the production and decay of heavy elements in astrophysical environments.

I used Fortran-based nuclear shell-model calculations to generate theoretical inputs for astrophysical applications. I also developed Python tools to organize, process, and analyze the resulting numerical data.

My contributions included:

- Running and modifying Fortran programs for nuclear shell-model calculations.
- Developing Python scripts to generate, organize, and manage large collections of model outputs.
- Building structured databases for shell-model calculation results.
- Analyzing calculated nuclear properties relevant to the decay of heavy elements in astrophysical processes.
- Creating visualization and data-processing tools for comparing results across nuclear configurations.

**Methods and tools:** Fortran, Python, nuclear shell model, scientific data analysis, database generation, computational nuclear astrophysics.

## University of Washington

### CMB-S4: Probing New Physics with the Cosmic Microwave Background({{ '/research/cmb-s4/' | relative_url }})

*March 2022–June 2023*  
**Advisor:** Prof. Marilena LoVerde

This project investigated how future cosmic microwave background observations could constrain primordial gravitational waves and other possible signatures of new physics.

I used the public Boltzmann code CLASS to calculate cosmic microwave background angular power spectra and examine how changes to primordial physics affect observable temperature and polarization signals.

My contributions included:

- Using CLASS to calculate CMB temperature and polarization power spectra.
- Studying how sources of new physics modify predicted CMB observables.
- Developing Python methods to generate primordial scalar and tensor power spectra.
- Investigating models with different tensor-to-scalar ratios.
- Forecasting constraints on primordial gravitational waves using Fisher-matrix methods.
- Working with Markov Chain Monte Carlo outputs to study parameter constraints and degeneracies.
- Participating in weekly Snowmass cosmology meetings and discussions.

**Methods and tools:** Python, CLASS, cosmological perturbation theory, CMB power spectra, Fisher matrices, MCMC, statistical forecasting.

### DAMIC-M: Dark Matter in CCDs at Modane

*January 2021–January 2022*  
**Advisor:** Prof. Alvaro Chavarria

DAMIC-M is a direct-detection experiment designed to search for low-mass dark matter using highly sensitive silicon charge-coupled devices.

My work focused on CCD characterization, detector noise reduction, and charge-collection performance.

My contributions included:

- Developing procedures to identify and reduce electrical noise in CCD systems.
- Using Linux-based systems and Arduino hardware to test detector components.
- Analyzing CCD images with Python and ROOT.
- Studying detector noise distributions and identifying possible sources of excess noise.
- Measuring charge-collection efficiency through image analysis.
- Comparing the performance of a CCD packaged with a pitch adapter against a conventionally packaged reference CCD.

**Methods and tools:** Python, ROOT, Linux, Arduino, CCD image analysis, detector characterization, noise analysis.
